Soft Stovetop Flatbread

Soft, fluffy flatbread cooked right on the stovetop with no oven required, brushed with melted butter and fresh herbs.

Tender, airy, and beautifully pocketed, this quick stovetop flatbread requires no oven or stand mixer. Made with a simple yeast dough enriched with warm milk and oil, each flatbread puffs on a hot griddle to create delicate golden brown spots. Finish with a brush of melted butter, chopped fresh parsley, and vibrant Aleppo pepper for an irresistible homemade side.

Why this works

Enriching the dough with milk and oil relaxes the gluten structure, yielding flatbreads that stay soft and flexible even after cooking quickly over direct stovetop heat.

Key ingredients

Instant Dry Yeast
Ensures quick rise and soft, pillowy texture.
Milk & Water Mix
Combining milk and water yields a softer crumb than water alone.
Aleppo Pepper
Adds mild fruity heat and vibrant red color to the finished flatbread.

Studio tips

  • Use medium heat so the interior cooks fully before the surface browns too quickly.
  • Brush with butter immediately after cooking to lock in softness and warmth.

Recipe card

Soft Stovetop Flatbread

Soft, fluffy flatbread cooked right on the stovetop with no oven required, brushed with melted butter and fresh herbs.

Difficulty: Easy 👌

20 min

Preparation

15 min

Cooking

1 h

Proofing

Utensils

  • Large glass mixing bowl
  • Wooden spoon or silicone spatula
  • Bench scraper
  • Rolling pin
  • Pastry brush
  • Non-stick griddle or skillet
  • Spatula
Total
1 h 35 min
Yield
6 flatbreads

Ingredients

Dough

  • 2/3 cup (160 ml) warm milk, not hot
  • 2/3 cup (160 ml) warm water, not hot
  • 1 tbsp (10 g) granulated sugar
  • 1.5 tbsp (10 g) instant dry yeast
  • 4 cups (500 g) all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 tbsp (8 g) fine salt
  • 3 tbsp (18 g) vegetable oil, plus extra for greasing bowl and pan

Topping & Finishing

  • 1 tbsp (14 g) butter, melted
  • 1 tbsp (3 g) fresh parsley, finely chopped
  • 1/2 tsp (1.5 g) Aleppo pepper, or red pepper flakes

Instructions

Prepare the Dough

  1. 1In a large glass bowl, combine warm milk and warm water (ensure liquid is warm to the touch, not hot).
  2. 2Add sugar and instant dry yeast to the liquid. Whisk or stir well with a wooden spoon until completely dissolved and no grains remain.
  3. 3Add the flour and salt to the liquid mixture. Stir with a spoon or spatula until a shaggy dough forms, then begin kneading by hand.
  4. 4Pour in 3 tablespoons of oil and continue to press and fold the dough until smooth, soft, and elastic.

Proof the Dough

  1. 5Grease the bottom and sides of the bowl with oil, return dough ball to the bowl, and lightly brush the surface with oil.
  2. 6Cover tightly with plastic wrap and a clean kitchen towel. Let rest in a warm spot for about 1 hour until doubled in volume.

Divide and Roll

  1. 7Punch down the proofed dough to release excess air. Turn dough out onto a lightly floured surface.
  2. 8Using a dough scraper, divide the dough into 6 equal portions.
  3. 9Tuck and roll each portion into a tight, smooth round ball. Cover dough balls with a towel to prevent drying out.
  4. 10Dust rolling surface with flour. Use a rolling pin to roll each dough ball out into an even, thin round disk.

Cook on Stovetop

  1. 11Heat a flat griddle or non-stick skillet over medium heat and brush lightly with a few drops of oil.
  2. 12Place rolled dough onto the heated pan. Cook until air bubbles puff up across the surface and the underside develops light golden spots.
  3. 13Flip flatbread and cook second side until nicely browned and puffed through. Repeat for remaining flatbreads.

Finish and Serve

  1. 14Stack hot flatbreads on a serving plate. Generously brush melted butter over top.
  2. 15Garnish with finely chopped fresh parsley and Aleppo pepper before serving warm.

Notes

  • Keep rolled dough disks covered under a towel while waiting to cook so they stay soft and supple.
  • Ensure liquids are warm (around 100°F–110°F) to activate yeast without killing it.

Frequently asked

Do I need an oven or stand mixer?

No, this recipe is designed to be kneaded by hand and cooked on a simple stovetop skillet or griddle.

Can I substitute Aleppo pepper?

Yes, crushed red pepper flakes or paprika can be substituted for mild heat and color.
